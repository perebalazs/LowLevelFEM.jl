Laminated Composite Shells

This tutorial extends the displacement–rotation shell formulation to a five-ply orthotropic laminate. Each ply has its own fiber orientation, and its constitutive stiffness is rotated into the global shell axes before integration through the thickness. The rectangular midsurface is generated with structured_rect_mesh; no dedicated composite shell element or multipoint constraints are required.

Example

Laminated composite shell notebook

The 200 × 80 mm cantilever uses five 0.5 mm plies with the stacking sequence [0°, 45°, 90°, −45°, 0°]. The lamina Young's moduli are 135 GPa along the fibers and 10 GPa across them. The left edge is clamped, and a total tensile force of 2000 N and bending moment of 600 N·mm are distributed along the right edge.

Through-thickness integration and weak form

The membrane, membrane–bending coupling and bending matrices are obtained by integrating the rotated plane-stress stiffness of each ply. With Qlay containing the rotated ply matrices and z the layer interfaces, the code follows the laminate stiffness moments directly:

A_lam = sum(∫(ζ -> Qlay[k],     z[k], z[k+1]) for k in eachindex(Qlay))
B_lam = sum(∫(ζ -> ζ*Qlay[k],   z[k], z[k+1]) for k in eachindex(Qlay))
D_lam = sum(∫(ζ -> ζ^2*Qlay[k], z[k], z[k+1]) for k in eachindex(Qlay))
D_mb = [A_lam B_lam; B_lam D_lam]

The unsymmetric stacking sequence produces a nonzero coupling matrix B_lam. The combined membrane and bending stiffness is assembled using the existing shell operators and matrix chains; transverse shear uses a separate through-thickness stiffness and reduced quadrature.

Results and ply stress recovery

The notebook reconstructs the membrane and bending resultants, transverse shear forces and in-plane stresses in each ply's material axes. In particular, the fiber-direction stress is evaluated separately for every ply, rather than averaged through the thickness. The example illustrates the deformation and stress redistribution due to the stacking sequence without applying a composite failure criterion.

The saved 40 × 16 element solution gives an average loaded-edge extension of approximately 0.03024 mm and an out-of-plane displacement of −1.04725 mm, with rotation about both in-plane axes.
